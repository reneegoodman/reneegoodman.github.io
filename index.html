About Me

Renée Goodman

Physics · Neuroscience · Intelligence

I am a physicist by training with an interest in interdisciplinary problems. I completed a BSc in Biology and Physics from the University of Windsor in 2021 where I conducted research with the Rondeau-Gagné group on conjugated polymers for flexible electronics. Following my bachelor's degree I had a brief interlude as a physicist for Thermtest Inc. where I worked on developing instruments for measuring the thermal properties of material.

In 2023 I started my MSc in experimental condensend matter physics at McGill University with Prof. Guillaume Gervais. My thesis on Mass Flow and Noise Measurements in a Nanoelectrofluidic Device was completed in 2025. I have now switched to theoretical physics for my PhD, under the supervision of Prof. Viola Priesemann at the Max Planck Institute for Dynamics and Self-Organization, where I use tools from statistical physics and network theory to analyze large connectomics datasets, such as the Female Adult Fly Brain, to understand how brain networks are organized and how they process information.

In my spare time I love to lift weights, go bikepacking, knit, and care for my many houseplants.

Research

PhD: Connectomics and Network Organization

Ongoing work at the Max Planck Institute for Dynamics and Self-Organization uses statistical physics and network analysis to study how large-scale brain connectomes are organized and how network structure shapes computation.

MSc Thesis: Nanoelectrofluidic Mass Flow and Noise

Experimental study of He4 gas flow through an 80nm de Laval nozzle, achieving supersonic flow rates as a proof-of-principle toward experiments related to the Unruh effect.
